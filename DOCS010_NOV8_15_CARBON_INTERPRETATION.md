# Digital Gardens vs. Wonder Valley: source-resolved carbon audit

## What the 60% ratio actually meant

The reference comparison was **conditional Digital Gardens partial net 1.520 Mt CO2e/year** divided by a **hypothetical Wonder Valley 95% carbon-capture partial gas-plus-upstream-methane total 2.463 Mt CO2e/year** = 61.7%. It was **not** a comparison with an ordinary gas-fired plant.

On the **same annual electricity service**, the model's unabated Wonder Valley 57%-efficient combined cycle produces 12.597 Mt/year direct fossil CO2, and 14.109 Mt CO2e/year after an assumed 1% methane-only upstream proxy. Digital Gardens conditional partial net is 10.8% of the latter, and partial gross is 14.3%. These two ratios **cannot be published as whole-project lifecycle reductions**, because the emission categories are not identical.

## Why a no-fossil-fuel-at-site project still has a nonzero GHG inventory

Digital Gardens proposes to run on renewable HVO/B100 rather than fossil diesel or natural gas. The calculated annual selected inventory contains: wind 0.231, solar 0.624, battery 0.117, grid imports 0.519, HVO 0.507, and B100 0.019 Mt CO2e. The combined selected lifecycle proxy is 2.017 Mt; subtracting hypothetical avoided household heating and drying 0.497 Mt produces 1.520 Mt.

This ledger does not report the mass of biogenic CO2 physically emitted from HVO/B100 combustion. That physical CO2 is **not zero**. Under biofuel GHG accounting conventions it may be reported separately as biogenic while upstream emissions are included and biogenic uptake/combustion must be consistently treated, avoiding double counting. HVO pathway intensity is unverified and depends on feedstock/process energy and land-use effects. It is **not legitimate** to set renewable fuel upstream emissions to zero just because the fuel is labelled renewable. Likewise wind, PV and battery supply chains and grid imports are not automatically carbon free.

The original generic factors are assumptions: grid 100 g CO2e/kWh; HVO 25 g CO2e/MJ; solar 35 g/kWh; wind 11 g/kWh; battery 70 kg CO2e/kWh amortised over 15 years. These are not metered field values or verified project supplier declarations. A full ISO-style LCA for either project has not been completed.

## Reporting policy for the comparative research article

Use the no-capture combined-cycle case as the primary **energy-generation** comparator. Explicitly display 90% and 95% CCS as planned/hypothetical cases; Wonder Valley states that it intends carbon capture and district heat (Gensler project description). Do not imply CCS will fail; do not assume it works at 95% or 100% before operational verification. In every CCS case, apply methane and fuel input adjustments consistently. Keep Digital Gardens gross emissions, local conditional displacement and partial net in different chart elements. Keep heat and jobs as actual research themes, not marginalised background.
