# Preliminary BC network load-flow and N−1 contingency screen

This is a **synthetic, unverified transmission expansion**, not a model of BC Hydro's actual network, and not an interconnection or NERC-compliance study. Its purpose is to test whether the manuscript's pooled electricity dispatch can be assumed deliverable at all 17 communities.

Input: 17 published town proxies, site-optimised SANY 8 MW capacity (5.856 GW wind, 4.866 GW PV), 36.00486 TWh/year campus electricity. Load distributed equally across 17 nodes (average 243.4 MW/node); six static operating states.

Architecture (all **assumed and entirely new**): seven 500-kV hub proxies, 500-kV mesh with 2 circuits per listed route (1900 MVA each), seven 500/230 kV substations with three 1250 MVA transformers each, 17 dual-circuit 230-kV 550 MVA feeder pairs, five doubled 500-kV wind connections (1400 MVA/circuit), and three grid gateways with two 2600 MVA circuits each. Lengths = geodesic x1.2 for HV routes, geodesic x1.18 (10-km floor) for 230-kV feeders. No project-specific rights-of-way, transformer contracts, line thermal studies, transmission-service arrangements, reactive plant or station drawings exist.

Active-power DC load flow: linear Kirchhoff susceptance model, each line/transformer capacity limited independently; scipy.optimize.linprog obtains constrained post-contingency corrective redispatch, curtailment, grid net imports/exports, HVO, and unmet load. IEEE-style voltage and branch ratings are **screening assumptions**, not BC Hydro planning criteria. Balanced positive-sequence steady-state AC power flow is solved independently using scipy.optimize.root with PQ buses and a common external GRID slack bus (no realistic frequency, governor, inverter reactive limits, tap controls, shunts, charging currents, faults or dynamic stability). No AC feasibility claim may be made when an AC solve fails or voltage limits are violated. No electrical losses were charged into LP dispatch (AC losses are separately reported). 500-kV corridors approximate R/X = .026/.30 ohm/km; 230-kV feeders .075/.38; transformer .004+j.08 pu on 1000 MVA base. See source for full inputs.

N−1 removes *each assumed line circuit or transformer individually* and reoptimizes dispatch immediately (optimistic remedial action). Failure to deliver after a contingency is reported as unserved MW. Simultaneous terminal faults, station outages, two-circuit shared-tower faults, loss of entire grid bus, fault-level/synchronism, generator-loss and inertia studies are not certified here. Sensitivities: connected average, winter peak/low renewables under 1/2.5/4 GW assumed imports, solar/wind export at 3 GW assumed limit, and complete grid-disconnection (grid-import/export forced to zero). Generation availability is static and uniformly scaled, not an additional 16-year hourly spatial-power study.

External-grid interchange is injected ONLY at the GRID equivalent (single slack terminal) and uses separate nonnegative import/export variables; imports cost C$125/MWh and exports receive an assumed C$50/MWh if an offtake agreement exists. Its power must flow across the individually rated gateway circuits: grid purchase is not placed directly on a local hub bus, and the system-wide Kirchhoff balance is enforced explicitly. The island case removes all GRID branches and sets external exchange to zero. Import/export ceilings are capacity sensitivities, not BC Hydro confirmed access. Local loads are always served first and export is capped; excess is curtailed. Export is never assumed to be compensated at retail customer rates.

Five additional plant-collector loss stresses remove the entire modeled production of one of the five wind regions, one at a time. These are severe grouped-plant tests rather than a standard individual 8-MW turbine N−1 outage.

Importantly the external grid equivalent does not represent a secured BC Hydro interconnection. External power imports are **not verified 100%-renewable supply**, and zero model emergency-gas generation cannot be inferred from grid imports. A distinct 16-year pooled bidirectional-grid dispatch is provided in hourly_grid_exchange.py; it is not chronologically coupled to the nodal AC/DC screen and does not incorporate line congestion, losses, or site-specific import restrictions.

References (screening context): BC Hydro provincial transmission maps https://www.bchydro.com/energy-in-bc/operations/transmission/transmission-system/maps.html ; NERC TPL-001-5.1 https://www.nerc.com/pa/Stand/Reliability%20Standards/TPL-001-5.1.pdf . Neither source validates any proposed corridor or transformer ratings. The static nodal network screen does not dispatch the selected battery; a simulated restricted-import winter shortfall is therefore not a proof of loss of load after battery dispatch.

The static network screens do **not** dispatch the proposed battery; this conservative winter snapshot is not a proof of actual loss of load at restricted import limits, because stored energy may bridge short deficits. AC checks use synthetic PQ buses and no compensating VAR controls.
